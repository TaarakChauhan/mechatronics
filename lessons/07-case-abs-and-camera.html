Module 7 — Deep integrated systems

Case studies: ABS & camera

Two deep cases show the same mechatronic spine: sense → condition → decide → actuate → verify, under hard constraints.

Case A — ABS / ESC family

Need: maintain steerability and stability during braking and yaw disturbances. Sensors: wheel-speed (often tone ring + Hall/VR), yaw rate and lateral acceleration (MEMS), steering angle, brake pressure. Conditioning: speed decoding, plausibility checks, sensor fusion for vehicle state. Control: detect impending lock (wheel deceleration / slip), modulate hydraulic pressure via solenoid valves and pump; ESC adds selective braking and engine torque requests. Actuators: hydraulic modulator. Watch-outs: false interventions, low-µ surfaces, spare-tire radius mismatches, communication loss to yaw sensor.

Figure: ABS as a high-rate mechatronic feedback loop.

Case B — Autofocus and image stabilisation

Need: sharp images despite subject distance changes and handshake. Sensors: contrast metrics from image sensor, phase-detect pixels, gyro/accelerometer for shake. Actuators: voice-coil motors (VCM) or piezoelectric elements moving lens groups; sometimes sensor-shift. Control: search or closed-loop focus; stabilisation uses feedforward from gyros plus feedback from position sensors on the lens barrel. Watch-outs: hunting in low contrast, thermal focus shift, latency between gyro and actuator.

Worked example — slip threshold intuition

If vehicle reference speed is 20 m/s and a wheel speed drops to 14 m/s, longitudinal slip ratio ≈ (20−14)/20 = 0.3. ABS aims to keep slip near values that still provide high friction — not zero rotation necessarily. Exact targets vary with surface estimation.

Then vs now ABS began as analogue/early digital hydraulic modulation. Modern ESC stacks fuse IMU networks, steering feel actuators, and drive-by-wire torque vectoring on EVs.

Shared lessons from both cases

Both ABS and camera AF/OIS demand low latency, careful filtering (not too much lag), and fault detection. Both blend feedforward (driver brake intent; gyro shake prediction) with feedback. Both degrade gracefully: ABS may revert to unmanaged brakes with warnings; cameras may disable OIS and rely on shorter exposure.

As systems evolve to EVs and computational photography, the mechatronic spine remains. New actuators (regen blending; sensor-shift) attach to the same sense–decide–actuate narrative.

Verification stories

ABS verification uses low-µ surfaces, split-µ, and sensor fault insertions. Camera OIS verification uses shaker tables and carefully lit focus charts. Both industries teach that scenario coverage beats single happy-path demos.

Trace requirements to tests: each plausibility rule needs a fault-injection case. Untested diagnostics are décor.

Field data feedback improves both systems — tyre variants and lens batches shift behaviour. Design update pipelines ethically and safely.

Notice how both cases monetise reliability: liability and brand for brakes; image quality reputation for cameras.

Extended studio notes — 07 case abs and camera

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Hard real-time sensing with fault detection is the shared DNA of both cases. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming perfect wheel radius makes spare-tyre scenarios dangerous for ABS. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Camera AF hunting in low contrast needs timeouts and fallback focus distances. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Trace each diagnostic to a test case in the verification matrix. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Modern ESC and computational photography extend these loops with richer sensors. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Slip s=(v−rω)/v. With v=15 m/s and rω=12 m/s, s=0.2. If the target slip band is 0.08–0.15 on that surface estimate, ABS should release/reapply toward the band — quantify rather than narrate “pumping brakes.”
