Project A · Sense and display

Build the smallest honest sensing chain: one microcontroller, one sensor, and a serial plot you can trust. Document noise and a simple filter. This project borrows the spirit of Georgia Tech sensor interfacing labs and ETH early instrumentation habits without copying their handouts.

Success criteria

  • Sensor reading streams at a stated sample rate.
  • Raw and filtered traces appear on the same plot.
  • You record noise amplitude (peak to peak or RMS) before and after filtering.
  • A one page note explains units, scaling, and one failure mode you observed.
Suggested bill of materials Any Module 5 class MCU board, one sensor (temperature, distance, or reflectance), USB serial to a plotter (Serial Plotter, PlotJuggler, or a Python script), jumper wires, and a notebook for the assumption list.

Procedure sketch

  1. Predict the expected signal range and noise source.
  2. Read the sensor with a blocking then a timed loop; compare jitter.
  3. Apply a simple moving average or first order low pass in firmware.
  4. Plot raw versus filtered. Change one physical disturbance (fan, tap, light) and capture it.
  5. Write the acceptance note with firmware identity and plot screenshots.

Worked failure · units lie

A student plots ADC counts and tunes a filter that looks beautiful. Later they convert to millimetres with the wrong scale factor and the control project inherits a silent gain error. Fix: convert to engineering units once, early, and plot those units forever after.

Stretch Add a second sensor and show them disagree during a known disturbance. That disagreement foreshadows Module 9 fusion humility.

Key terms

Serial plot
A live graph of numeric samples from the MCU.
Moving average
Simple FIR style smoother over the last N samples.
Engineering units
Physical units after scaling, not raw counts.
Sample rate
How often you claim to measure; verify it.
