Module 8 — What changed: modern systems

Sensors: then vs now

The measurement principles you learned still hold. The packaging, intelligence, and networking of sensors changed the architecture of machines.

MEMS, ToF, vision, LiDAR

MEMS IMUs put gyros and accelerometers on grams of silicon — enabling drones, phones, and ESC. Time-of-flight sensors measure distance with modulated light. Cameras and LiDAR became “sensors” in the mechatronic BOM, not exotic research gear, bringing calibration, lighting, and compute burdens.

Sensor fusion

Fusion (complementary filters, Kalman/EKF/UKF) combines imperfect sensors into better state estimates: wheel odometry + IMU + vision for mobile robots; encoder + load torque observer for drives.

IO-Link vs 4–20 mA

Analogue 4–20 mA remains robust and ubiquitous. IO-Link adds digital identity, parameterisation, and diagnostics over short standard cables — smart sensors that can tell you they are dirty or out of range, not just “12.4 mA.”

Key idea Smart sensors move conditioning and diagnostics into the field device — your PLC receives meaning, not only milliamps.

Worked example — fusion intuition

IMU yaw integrates gyro with drift; magnetometer is absolute but noisy. A complementary filter trusts gyro for fast changes and magnetometer for long-term heading — bandwidth separation as design tool.

Key terms

MEMS
Micro-electromechanical systems.
IO-Link
Point-to-point smart sensor/actuator digital protocol.
Sensor fusion
Combining multiple sensing modalities for better state.

Calibration and identity

Smart sensors store calibration and can be hot-swapped with identity retained via IO-Link or similar. That changes maintenance: replace the device, download parameters, confirm diagnostics. Cybersecurity follows — a writable sensor is also an attack surface.

Vision and LiDAR force extrinsic calibration (where is the sensor on the robot?) into the mechatronic BOM. A loose mount is a software bug that looks like a perception failure.

Fusion implementation notes

Start with complementary filters before full EKF if your states are simple; fewer parameters, easier debugging.

Health monitoring of fusion: if residuals grow, distrust the estimate and degrade control.

Smart sensor firmware updates need the same change control as PLC logic.

Keep analogue fallback strategies for brownfield upgrades where digital hubs fail.

Extended studio notes — 08 sensors then vs now

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Smart sensors move diagnostics fieldward; they do not repeal calibration physics. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming fusion always improves estimates ignores correlated failures (all GPS-denied indoors). Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Extrinsic calibration of cameras is a mechanical stability problem. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Brownfield 4–20 mA can coexist with IO-Link during staged upgrades. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Security of writable field devices is now part of sensor selection. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Complementary filter: gyro integrates fast; magnetometer corrects slow drift with low-pass weight α=0.02 per sample at 100 Hz. Tune α so magnetic disturbances do not yank heading while still bounding gyro bias growth.
