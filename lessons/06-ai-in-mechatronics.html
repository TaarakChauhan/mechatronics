Module 6 — Models and control

AI in mechatronics

“AI in mechatronics” historically meant rule-based and soft-computing methods that encoded operator skill when crisp models were weak. Today it also means machine learning — but the classical bridge still matters.

Perception and cognition (classical framing)

Sensors perceive; algorithms decide. Fuzzy logic maps crisp measurements into membership degrees (“somewhat hot”), applies rules, and defuzzifies to a crisp actuator command — useful for nonlinear or linguistic control where PID struggles.

Figure: Triangular membership functions for cool / warm / hot — original teaching sketch.

Worked example — membership

At 28 °C, suppose “warm” membership µw=0.7 and “hot” µh=0.2. A rule “if hot then strong cool fan” contributes less than “if warm then medium fan” at this point. Defuzzification (e.g. centroid) blends rule outputs into one duty cycle.

Then vs now Fuzzy and expert systems were the 1990s–2000s “AI” chapter. In 2026, vision networks, TinyML anomaly detectors, and learned feedforward sit beside — not always instead of — PID and fuzzy supervisors (Module 8).

Key terms

Membership function
Maps crisp value to degree in a fuzzy set.
Defuzzification
Converts fuzzy conclusions to crisp actuation.

Bridging to modern ML carefully

Fuzzy supervisors still excel as readable outer layers (“if vibration high then reduce feed”). Learned models shine when features are rich and labelled data exist — vision defects, acoustic anomalies. Hybrid designs freeze a certified inner loop and allow learning only in advisory or slowly adapted feedforward paths.

Validation remains non-negotiable: distribution shift (new product variant, new lighting) breaks naive networks. Mechatronic ML inherits all classical sensor and timing disciplines.

Governance of intelligent features

Any learned component needs a defined fallback: last PID-only mode, reduced speed, or safe stop. Intelligence without fallback is fragility.

Keep training data provenance. Models trained on one machine may not transfer without domain adaptation.

Prefer interpretable outer supervisors when operators must trust interventions. Black-box torque commands on safety-critical axes demand exceptional assurance cases.

Measure compute latency budgets the same way you measure sample time — ML inference is part of the delay chain.

Extended studio notes — 06 ai in mechatronics

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Readable supervisors earn operator trust; opaque commands need stronger assurance. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming training distributions cover winter/summer plants causes seasonal surprises. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Inference latency belongs on the same budget sheet as sample time. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Fallback modes must be tested as thoroughly as the smart path. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. TinyML in Module 8 is this lesson’s descendant with better tooling. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Membership: at 35°C, µ_hot=0.6, µ_warm=0.3. Rule weights for cooling fan duties 80% and 40% blend roughly to (0.6·80+0.3·40)/(0.6+0.3)≈67% duty before finer defuzzification methods — enough to see graded control action.
