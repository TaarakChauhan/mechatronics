Module 4 — Actuation

Fluid power

Fluid power transmits force through pressurised fluids. Pneumatics uses compressed air — clean, fast, compliant, modest force density. Hydraulics uses oil — high force density, stiff actuation, messier and heavier infrastructure.

Core components

  • Directional control valves (DCV) — steer fluid to extend/retract cylinders (e.g. 4/2, 5/3 spool valves).
  • Pressure valves — relief, reducing, sequence.
  • Cylinders — single/double acting; force ≈ P·A (minus losses).
  • Servo / proportional valves — continuous metering for closed-loop motion or pressure.
  • Process valves — globe, ball, butterfly with pneumatic/electric actuators for flow control in plants.
Figure: Conceptual cylinder driven through a directional valve from pressure (P) and tank (T).

Worked example — cylinder force

Bore diameter 50 mm → area ≈ π(0.025)2 = 1.96×10−3 m2. At 6 bar (0.6 MPa) gauge, ideal extend force ≈ 0.6×106 × 1.96×10−3 ≈ 1180 N. Rod-side area is smaller, so retract force is less at the same pressure.

Watch-out Air is compressible: pneumatic position control without feedback is imprecise. Hydraulics can be violently stiff — trapped pressure is an energy hazard.
Then vs now On/off pneumatics still rule pick-and-place. Servo-hydraulics and electrohydrostatic actuators compete with high-torque electric drives; energy efficiency and leak reduction drive redesigns.

Energy, heat, and control valves

Hydraulic power wasted across a relief valve becomes heat — coolers are not optional decoration. Efficient architectures use load sensing and variable pumps. Pneumatic systems leak; energy audits often find compressors fighting holes.

Process control valves are characterised by inherent and installed flow characteristics (linear, equal percentage). The installed curve depends on the piping system. Controllers “detuned in the field” are often fighting a nonlinear installed valve — a mechatronic (process + actuator) issue.

Sizing and dynamic behaviour

Cylinder speed depends on flow and area: v = Q/A (ideal). Undersized hoses and fittings starve actuators and create pressure drops mistaken for pump failure. Size the whole path.

Accumulators store energy and stiffen systems; they also change dynamics. Including accumulator capacitance in models explains otherwise mysterious low-frequency modes.

Servo-hydraulics can outperform electrics on force density, but leakage and fluid maintenance are lifecycle costs. Total cost of ownership belongs in the mechatronic trade study.

Safety: blocked cylinders under pressure store energy. Lockout procedures and pressure-relief verification are part of actuation design documentation.

Extended studio notes — 04 fluid power

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Pressure times area is only the start; flow and heat decide whether the motion sustains. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming incompressible oil is fine until aeration and hose expansion appear. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Servo valves demand cleanliness codes that facilities ignore at their peril. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Lockout of stored hydraulic energy is an actuation design deliverable. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Electric alternatives later compete on efficiency — compare honestly with duty cycles. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Need 0.4 m/s extend on 40 mm bore (A≈1.26e-3 m²). Ideal flow Q=vA≈5.0e-4 m³/s ≈ 30 L/min. Select pump/valve path for 30 L/min plus margin; a 15 L/min valve will simply never meet the cycle time regardless of pressure.
