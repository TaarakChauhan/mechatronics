Module 8 — What changed: modern systems

ROS 2 and modern software

Robotics software matured from bespoke bare-metal piles into distributed frameworks. ROS 2 (Robot Operating System 2) is the dominant open ecosystem for research and increasingly for industry-adjacent systems.

Nodes, topics, services, actions

Nodes are processes. Topics stream messages (sensor data). Services are request/response. Actions handle long goals with feedback (e.g. “navigate there”). DDS middleware supplies discovery and QoS (reliability, durability, deadline).

ros2_control and simulation-first

ros2_control abstracts actuators/sensors for controllers. Simulation (Gazebo / Ignition / other) lets you test planning and perception before hardware — digital rehearsal as a lifestyle. Hardware-in-the-loop still required for timing and electrical truth.

Then vs now Bare-metal assembly ISRs for every behaviour → layered stacks: RTOS/drive firmware beneath; ROS 2 for coordination and perception; safety PLCs remaining sovereign over estops.

Worked example — QoS mismatch

A lidar topic publishes best-effort; a safety perception node expects reliable delivery. Under Wi-Fi loss, the safety node starves. Aligning DDS QoS is a systems engineering task, not a checkbox.

Key terms

DDS
Data Distribution Service — ROS 2 middleware substrate.
QoS
Quality of service policies for communications.
ros2_control
Hardware interface / controller framework in ROS 2.

Deployment and lifecycle

ROS 2 shines in perception and coordination; it does not replace drive firmware. Bridges (ros2_control hardware interfaces) must honour real-time budgets. Lifecycle nodes clarify inactive/active/finalised states — cousins of PLC equipment modes.

Security: DDS without authentication on a factory floor is naïve. Treat robot networks as OT: segment, authenticate, and monitor.

Realtime bridging

Run hard loops off ROS when deadlines are sub-millisecond; bridge setpoints at a safe rate.

Use fixed frame IDs and documented TF trees; broken frames cause “haunted” robots.

CI should run simulation smoke tests on every merge. Hardware-in-loop nightly catches timing issues simulation misses.

Containerise non-realtime stacks carefully so startup order remains deterministic.

Extended studio notes — 08 ros2 and software

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Frameworks accelerate robotics only if realtime boundaries stay sacred. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming best-effort lidar topics feed safety perception is a QoS mistake. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Simulation reduces risk but does not prove timing under interrupt load. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Lifecycle states make shutdown and fault recovery explicit. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Twins and edge AI next consume the data these nodes publish. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Action server “dock” fails if battery topic is stale >1 s — encode that as a precondition rather than navigating into a dead robot against a charger.
