Module 8 — What changed: modern systems

Digital twins & edge AI

Digital twins and edge AI are the 2026 headline pair: a living model plus local intelligence that does not wait for the cloud round-trip.

Digital twins (2026 sense)

A twin is more than a CAD file. Live twins synchronise with plant telemetry, expose what-if simulation, and — increasingly — use generative scenario search to explore failure spaces. Fidelity must match the decision: thermal twins for ovens, kinematic twins for robots, discrete-event twins for logistics.

Figure: Physical ↔ twin ↔ decision loop with live sync.

Edge AI / TinyML

Models once needing GPUs now distill onto MCUs (TinyML) for keyword spotting, vibration anomaly detection, and vision triggers. Predictive maintenance uses current and vibration signatures to forecast bearing or tool failure — classical signal features plus learned classifiers.

Worked example — PdM feature

Motor current FFT shows rising sideband energy at bearing fault frequency. A tiny classifier on the drive’s MCU flags “inspect within 72 h” without streaming raw audio to the cloud — bandwidth and privacy win.

Data pipelines and trust

Twins die when timestamps lie or units disagree. Invest in semantic models (OPC UA companion specs help) so “motor_temp” means the same thing everywhere. Edge AI models need versioning, rollback, and monitoring for drift — MLOps meets OT change control.

Generative scenario search is powerful for exploring rare faults but must be constrained by physics and safety envelopes. A twin that proposes an illegal valve sequence is a hazard generator.

What to twin first

Twin the bottleneck asset, not the entire factory mural. A spindle health twin with proven ROI beats a shallow plant-wide vanity model.

Define sync rate and allowed desync alarms. A twin lagging by hours is a historian, not a twin.

For TinyML, budget flash/RAM and quantisation-aware training. Measure inference time on target silicon.

Close the loop to CMMS work orders so predictions create actions humans track.

Extended studio notes — 08 digital twins and edge ai

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Twin value equals decision improvement, not polygon count. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming unlimited uplink for raw vibration wastes bandwidth and leaks IP. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Model versioning and rollback are OT change-control cousins. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Start with one high-ROI asset twin before enterprise murals. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Safety/security lesson next protects the connected plant you just imagined. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

MCU classifier runs in 3 ms on 1 kHz vibration frames; alert PLC via boolean over cyclic I/O. If inference occasionally overruns to 12 ms, drop to last-good classification rather than blocking the I/O task — deadline over accuracy for that frame.
