Module 6 — Models and control

Closed-loop control & PID

PID control remains the workhorse of industrial regulation: simple, tunable, and well understood — when implemented with eyes open.

On/off, P, I, D

On/off (bang-bang) switches about a threshold — simple, oscillatory. Proportional reduces error but leaves steady-state offset for many plants. Integral removes offset by accumulating error. Derivative anticipates by sensing rate of change — noisy if undifferentiated raw measurements.

Digital PID

Sample period Ts: a common positional form uses discrete sums and differences. Always consider derivative filtering, integral anti-windup when actuators saturate, and setpoint weighting.

/* Original discrete PID sketch (teaching) */
e = r - y;
integral += e * Ts;
if (integral > Imax) integral = Imax;
if (integral < -Imax) integral = -Imax;
deriv = (e - e_prev) / Ts;
u = Kp*e + Ki*integral + Kd*deriv;
e_prev = e;

Worked example — Ziegler–Nichols style intuition

Raise Kp in pure P control until sustained oscillation at ultimate gain Ku with period Tu. Classic tables set PID parameters as fractions of Ku and Tu. Use as a starting point, not scripture — then refine for overshoot and robustness.

Then vs now Single-loop PID still rules base layers. Outer loops may use MPC, learning-based feedforward, or adaptive gains — layered on the same plants.

Key terms

Anti-windup
Stopping integral growth during saturation.
Ultimate gain Ku
Proportional gain at sustained oscillation boundary.

Practical digital PID details

Derivative on measurement (not on error) avoids setpoint kicks. Setpoint weighting lets proportional act fully on feedback while softening setpoint steps. Sampling jitter and output quantisation (especially with slow PWM) limit how large Kd can be.

Cascade outer position / inner velocity / innermost current assigns roles: current loop enforces torque; velocity damps; position tracks. Tune inside-out. Adaptive and gain-scheduled PID change parameters with operating point (speed, load) when one set of gains cannot cover the envelope.

Commissioning sequence

Tune inner loops first with outer loops open or in manual. Confirm current loop bandwidth, then velocity, then position or process variable.

Use setpoint steps small enough to avoid saturation when estimating linear behaviour; then test large steps to verify anti-windup.

Record before/after trends with identical disturbances. Subjective “it feels better” is not a commissioning record.

Schedule periodic retuning checks after mechanical wear or process changes; gains are not eternal.

Extended studio notes — 06 closed loop and pid

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Saturation is normal; controllers that deny it misbehave. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming one global gain set works across loads invites fragile production. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Derivative filtering time constants are first-class tuning knobs. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Commissioning records should include step responses, not only final gains. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Learning feedforwards later should sit beside, not replace, this competence. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.
