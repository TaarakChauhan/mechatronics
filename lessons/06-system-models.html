Module 6 — Models and control

System models

Models let us predict and design before metal is cut. Mechatronics uses lumped-parameter building blocks across domains.

Building blocks

  • Mechanical translation — mass, spring, damper.
  • Rotation — inertia, torsional spring, rotary damper.
  • Electrical — R, L, C.
  • Fluid — inertance, capacitance (compliance), resistance.
  • Thermal — capacitance (mcp), resistance (conduction/convection).

Coupling elements connect domains: gears (rotation–rotation), levers, voice-coils and motors (electro–mechanical), pistons (fluid–mechanical).

Figure: Classic second-order mechanical plant.
Key idea Linearity is an assumption with a validity region. Saturation, backlash, and stick–slip make real plants nonlinear — design with that humility.

Worked example — thermal lag

A small heater block: thermal capacitance Ct = 400 J/K, to ambient through Rt = 0.5 K/W. Time constant τ = RtCt = 200 s. A step in heater power moves temperature with a first-order lag of ~200 s — slow compared to electronics, crucial for tuning.

Key terms

Lumped model
Finite energy-storage elements approximating distributed physics.
Time constant
Characteristic lag scale for first-order systems.
Coupling
Element linking domains (e.g. motor constant).

Analogy and nondimensional thinking

The same second-order math appears in RLC circuits and mass–spring–dampers. Learning the analogies accelerates intuition: inductance ~ mass, capacitance ~ spring compliance (with care about conjugate variables). Nondimensional forms (ζ, ωn) let you compare plants across units.

Model reduction matters: a 20-state FEM model is useless for a 1 kHz embedded loop. Keep the poles near the control bandwidth and treat faster dynamics as parasitics or filtered noise.

Building models that stay useful

Start with conservation laws and constitutive equations, then linearise about an operating point. State the operating point explicitly; a linearised valve model at 10% open differs from one at 80% open.

Validate models with step tests and, if possible, frequency sweeps. If the model cannot predict the first overshoot within a factor of two, improve the model before fancy control.

Keep parameter uncertainty ranges. Robust control thinking begins with “K between 0.8 and 1.3,” not a fake single number.

Couple domains only as needed. An electro-mechanical motor model needs L, R, Kt, Ke, J, b — enough for current and speed loops without modelling every tooth fillet.

Extended studio notes — 06 system models

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Models are tools for decisions; fidelity beyond the decision wastes time. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming constant parameters across temperature ignores copper resistance rise and lubricant viscosity. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Unmodelled delay is the usual assassin of aggressively designed loops. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Publish parameter sources (datasheet, fit, guess) with confidence tags. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Twins later amplify both good and bad models — garbage in, cinematic garbage out. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Thermal: C=500 J/K, R=0.4 K/W, τ=200 s. Heater step 100 W ⇒ steady ΔT=IR-like = P·R=40 K. After 200 s, ~63% of 40 K ≈ 25 K rise — use to sanity-check plant tests before blaming the controller for “slow heating.”
