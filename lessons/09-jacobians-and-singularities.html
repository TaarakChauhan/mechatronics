Module 9 · Robot mechanics and estimation

Jacobians, velocity, and singularities

If forward kinematics answers where the tool is, the Jacobian answers how fast the tool moves when joints move. Mechatronic designers meet Jacobians whenever they size motors for Cartesian speeds, detect dangerous arm postures, or implement simple teleoperation that commands tool velocity instead of joint angles.

The mapping idea

Collect joint rates into a vector. Collect the end effector linear and angular velocity into another vector. For many serial robots, a matrix relates them locally. That matrix is the Jacobian at the current configuration. It changes as the arm moves. It is not a single constant gain.

Learning outcomes State what a Jacobian maps between joint rates and end effector velocity. Explain singularities as configurations where that map loses usable rank. Relate Jacobian thinking to resolved rate control and to force duality at a conceptual level.

Why singularities hurt

Near some postures the Jacobian becomes poorly conditioned. Large joint rates may be required for tiny Cartesian moves. Numerically, commands blow up. Mechanically, the arm loses a motion direction. Operators see the robot hesitate or refuse a path. Classical teaching examples include outstretched elbows and wrist alignments that line up axes.

Design responses include avoiding singular regions in the workspace, switching control representations, or slowing Cartesian commands when a manipulability measure drops.

Dual story: forces and torques

Through virtual work, the same geometry that maps velocities also relates end effector forces to joint torques. Intuition: a long outstretched arm needs different joint torque patterns than a folded arm for the same tip force. Motor sizing and collision safety both care.

Worked scenario · glue bead at constant tip speed

A three link planar arm must sweep a glue bead at constant tip speed. Near full extension, joint two approaches a singularity. The planner that ignored manipulability commands impossible joint rates. A better planner keeps a bent elbow, accepts a longer path, and keeps joint rates inside amplifier limits.

Studio lab idea In simulation (Modern Robotics software or a simple planar arm notebook), plot tip speed versus configuration for fixed joint rate norms. Mark configurations where the ratio collapses.

Key terms

Jacobian
Local map from joint rates to end effector velocity (twist).
Singularity
Configuration where the Jacobian loses usable rank or becomes poorly conditioned.
Resolved rate control
Converting desired tool velocity into joint rates via the Jacobian.
Manipulability
A measure of how easily the arm can move in Cartesian directions.
Twist
Combined linear and angular velocity of a body.
