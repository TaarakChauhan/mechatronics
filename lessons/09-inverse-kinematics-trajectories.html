Module 9 · Robot mechanics and estimation

Inverse kinematics and trajectory generation

Inverse kinematics (IK) asks the reverse of forward kinematics: given a desired tool pose, what joint angles achieve it? Trajectory generation asks how to move through joint space or task space over time without shocking the amplifiers. Together they turn teach points into motion that plants can actually run.

Analytic versus numeric IK

Some arms admit closed form solutions. A spherical wrist plus known link lengths can yield formulas you can code and audit. Many modern arms and redundant robots lean on numeric IK: iterate from a seed configuration until the FK error is small. Analytic solutions are fast and predictable when they exist. Numeric methods are flexible but need good seeds, joint limits, and singularity handling.

Learning outcomes Contrast analytic versus numeric IK. Describe cubic polynomial joint trajectories and via points. Explain why timing and limits turn a geometric path into a runnable trajectory.

Multiple solutions and preferences

Even simple planar arms often have elbow up and elbow down solutions for the same tip pose. Industrial cells pick a convention and stick to it. Changing branches mid motion can whip the elbow through a fixture. Document the preferred configuration family in the cell design package.

Trajectories and via points

A geometric path is an ordered set of poses or joint vectors. A trajectory adds time. Cubic polynomials on each joint between via points are a classical intro method: they match position and velocity at segment ends when formulated carefully. Higher order blends and trapezoidal speed profiles appear in CNC and robot controllers. The mechatronic constraint remains: torque, speed, and jerk limits must be respected or the tracking loop will saturate.

Worked scenario · pick pose with via points

A cobot must lift clear of a vise before translating sideways. A single joint space linear move clips the vise. Inserting a via point above the part lengthens the cycle by a fraction of a second and keeps the mesh clear. Timing each segment with modest peak speeds keeps the drives inside continuous torque ratings.

Studio lab idea For a two link planar arm, pick a reachable tip pose and find both elbow configurations on paper. Then design a three via point joint trajectory with zero start and end velocity using cubic segments conceptually (sketch position versus time).

Key terms

Inverse kinematics (IK)
Finding joint variables that achieve a desired end effector pose.
Analytic IK
Closed form solution from geometry and algebra.
Numeric IK
Iterative search from a seed configuration.
Via point
An intermediate pose or joint set the trajectory must pass.
Trajectory
A path with timing, not only geometry.
