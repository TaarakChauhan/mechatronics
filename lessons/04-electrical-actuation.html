Module 4 — Actuation

Electrical actuation

Electrical actuation spans from a relay contact to a field-oriented BLDC servo. Selection starts from motion profile, torque/force, duty cycle, environment, and control interface.

Switching and solenoids

Mechanical switches and relays offer galvanic isolation and simplicity; life and arcing limit them. Solid-state relays and MOSFETs switch silently and fast but need thermal design and may leak. Solenoids convert current to short-stroke force — great for valves and latches, poor for long precise strokes.

Motors

  • Brushed DC — easy drive; brush wear; still fine for cost-sensitive duties.
  • AC induction — workhorses with VFDs for pumps/fans.
  • Stepper — open-loop discrete moves; stalls if torque margin vanishes.
  • DC / BLDC servo — closed-loop with encoder; high dynamic performance.
Then vs now Brushed DC plus H-bridge was the teaching default. Production motion increasingly uses BLDC with FOC, integrated drives, and safety torque off (STO) — Module 8.

Worked example — stepper open-loop risk

A 1.8° stepper (200 steps/rev) with 16× microstepping commands 3200 pulses/rev. If load torque exceeds available torque at speed, the motor skips steps and position is lost without an encoder. Adding a cheap encoder closes the truth gap.

Key terms

VFD
Variable frequency drive for AC motors.
Servo motor
Motor operated in closed-loop motion control.
STO
Safe Torque Off — safety function removing torque-producing energy.

Thermal limits and selection workflow

Motors are thermally limited. Peak torque may be available for seconds; continuous torque is lower. Duty cycle, ambient, and altitude change the continuous rating. Selection workflow: motion profile → torque/speed envelope → margin for friction/acceleration → thermal check → feedback/resolution → drive/network interface → safety functions (STO, SS1, etc.).

Stepper open-loop is attractive until missed steps cost a scrap batch. Closed-loop steppers and cheap servo systems have narrowed that gap. Choose based on risk of silent position loss, not only catalogue price.

Drive interfaces and braking

Command interfaces evolved from ±10 V analogue velocity commands to fieldbus cyclic setpoints. Analogue still exists and still suffers offset and noise; digital setpoints need watchdog of communications loss.

Dynamic braking and regenerative handling protect DC buses. Vertical axes need mechanical brakes rated for load, not only electronic disable.

Inrush and EMC filters affect whether a drive trips neighbouring sensors. Layout and earthing plans are actuation documents.

Selection example: continuous torque 1.2 N·m at 2000 rpm, peak 3.5 N·m for 1 s accel. Check catalogue S1/S3 ratings and thermal models rather than peak-only shopping.

Extended studio notes — 04 electrical actuation

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Thermal limits turn peak torque fantasies into continuous-duty realities. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming steppers never lose steps is fine until a jam scrapes a fixture. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” STO wiring errors create either nuisance trips or silent loss of protection. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Brake sizing for gravity loads is a safety calculation, not an accessory checkbox. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. BLDC+FOC in Module 8 extends this selection workflow rather than deleting it. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Profile: accel 0.2 s to 1000 rpm, J_total 1e-3 kg·m². ω=104.7 rad/s, α=523 rad/s², T=Jα≈0.52 N·m plus friction 0.1 ⇒ ~0.62 N·m peak. Continuous may be only 0.25 N·m — verify catalogue peak time and RMS duty.
