Module 6 — Models and control

Frequency response & stability

Frequency-domain thinking asks: if we excite the plant with sinusoids, how do gain and phase shift with frequency? Bode plots make margins visible.

Bode idea

Magnitude (dB) and phase (degrees) versus log frequency. Crossing 0 dB with phase near −180° is dangerous. Gain margin and phase margin quantify distance from that brink.

Figure: Illustrative Bode magnitude with 0 dB reference and gain crossover mark.

Worked example — margin intuition

If at the gain crossover the phase is −120°, phase margin = 60° — generally comfortable for many loops. If phase is −170°, margin = 10° — expect overshoot and fragility to delay.

Watch-out Digital sampling and computation delay add phase lag that Bode analysis must include — ignoring delay is a classic instability source.

Key terms

Phase margin
Additional phase lag tolerable at gain crossover before −180°.
Gain margin
Factor by which gain may rise before instability at phase crossover.

Loop shaping intuition

Engineers raise crossover for speed, then add lead (phase boost) or lag (low-frequency gain) to repair margins and steady-state error. Notch filters carve out resonances. Every filter adds phase lag somewhere — there is no free lunch.

Nyquist’s criterion generalises Bode margins for tricky plants. For this course, internalise: stay away from the −1 point in the complex plane; margins quantify that distance in gain/phase language.

Measuring margins on real hardware

Inject a perturbation and measure loop gain where safe, or use relay feedback experiments carefully. Many drives offer built-in Bode tools — learn them.

Cable delays, filter delays, and computation delays stack. A 1 ms total delay at 50 Hz crossover costs about 18° phase — enough to ruin a thin margin.

Nonlinear friction can make small-signal margins look fine while large moves chatter. Validate with representative amplitudes.

Store Bode screenshots with firmware version and load condition; margins without context mislead the next engineer.

Extended studio notes — 06 frequency and stability

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Margins are oxygen for uncertainty; spend them knowingly. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming analogue Bode equals digital loop ignores sample delay and holds. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Notch filters that move with temperature need scheduled updates or wide notches. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Archive margin plots with load and firmware metadata. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. PID lead/lag design is loop shaping under another name. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Crossover 40 Hz with 0.8 ms delay ⇒ phase lag ≈ 360·40·0.0008=11.5°. If mechanical plant already leaves only 35° margin, delay eats a third of it — redesign compute or lower crossover.
