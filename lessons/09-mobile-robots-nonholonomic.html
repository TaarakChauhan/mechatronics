Module 9 · Robot mechanics and estimation

Mobile robots and nonholonomic constraints

Factory mechatronics often lives on fixed bases. Warehouses, campuses, and field robots add rolling contact. A differential drive base cannot slide sideways like an omnidirectional platform. That restriction is a nonholonomic constraint: it limits instantaneous velocity, not necessarily the reachable positions over time.

Differential drive kinematics

Two driven wheels with measured speeds determine forward velocity and yaw rate in the body frame. Integrating those rates (odometry) estimates pose in the map, until slip lies to you. Encoders alone are not a localisation system. They are a motion hint that fusion must humble.

Learning outcomes Explain differential drive kinematics at an intuition level. Contrast holonomic versus nonholonomic bases. List localisation building blocks that connect to Module 8 ROS navigation stories.

Holonomic versus nonholonomic

A holonomic base can command velocity in the plane freely (within actuator limits), including sideways. Mecanum and some omni designs approximate that behaviour. A nonholonomic differential drive must manoeuvre: parallel park style motions appear even though the workspace of positions looks open. Planners and controllers must respect the constraint or tracking errors grow.

Localisation building blocks

Dead reckoning, map matching (lidar or vision), beacons, and GPS class fixes each fail in different environments. Monte Carlo localisation maintains particles over a map. Loop closure in SLAM corrects drift when places are recognised again. Module 8 named autonomy stacks. This lesson names why those stacks exist.

Worked scenario · dock approach

An AMR must dock to a charger along a fixed corridor pose. Pure goal pose IK thinking from arms does not transfer. The base must arc into alignment because it cannot translate sideways at the last centimetre. A short sequence of forward and rotate primitives, guided by a reflector or AprilTag, finishes the dock within tolerance.

Studio lab idea On paper, derive body frame velocity from left and right wheel speeds for a differential drive with track width b. Then list three environments where wheel odometry alone will diverge, and which sensor you would fuse first.

Key terms

Differential drive
Two independently driven wheels that set forward speed and yaw rate.
Holonomic
Able to command the full velocity space of interest (within limits).
Nonholonomic
Instantaneous velocity is restricted (for example no sideways slide).
Odometry
Pose integration from wheel or inertial motion hints.
Monte Carlo localisation
Particle filter localisation on a known map.
