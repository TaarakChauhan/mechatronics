Module 8 — What changed: modern systems

Drives & actuators now

Actuation hardware shifted from brushed simplicity toward efficient, software-defined motion — while new materials appear at the edges.

BLDC + FOC

Brushless DC / PMSM machines with field-oriented control deliver smooth torque, high efficiency, and wide speed range. Many applications that once used brushed DC or stepper open-loop now use integrated servo drives.

Integrated drives and cobot joints

Drives move onto motors (decentralised). Collaborative robot joints combine torque sensing, compliant control, and safety-rated monitoring. Piezo and shape-memory alloy (SMA) actuators serve micro-positioning and specialised duties.

Watch-out FOC tunes many gains (current, speed, observers). “It spins” ≠ “it meets torque ripple and thermal specs.”

Worked example — why FOC replaced simple drives

A packaging turret needing low-speed high torque and quiet operation: microstepping was noisy and hot; a FOC BLDC with 20-bit encoder meets torque at stall-like speeds with less acoustic noise — at higher electronics cost.

Key terms

FOC
Field-oriented control of AC/BLDC machines.
STO
Safe Torque Off.
PMSM
Permanent-magnet synchronous machine.

Safety and energy

Modern drives expose STO, SS1, SLS, and related functions over safe fieldbuses. Mechanical brakes still matter for vertical axes. Regenerative braking returns energy or dumps it to resistors — thermal design again.

Compliant cobot actuation may use series elastic elements or software impedance control on stiff gears. Understanding classical stiffness helps you read those datasheets critically.

Commissioning FOC systems

Verify motor parameters (Rs, Ld, Lq, flux) used by the observer; wrong parameters cause torque ripple and heat.

Check encoder alignment offset for PMSM — a classic bring-up step.

Enable STO and test that torque ceases under fault injection, including wiring faults to the safety input.

Compare energy and acoustic noise against the legacy brushed solution to justify cost with data.

Extended studio notes — 08 drives and actuators now

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. FOC parameterisation quality decides whether “servo” means smooth or noisy. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming STO is tested once at FAT and never again invites latent wiring faults. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Cobot torque sensing changes integration risk assessments under TS 15066. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Compare lifecycle energy, not only purchase price, against legacy brushed systems. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Controllers and networks next decide how you command these drives. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Misaligned encoder offset of 10° electrical on a PMSM produces noticeable torque ripple and heating. Run auto-commission alignment routines and verify with a no-load current circle plot if available.

As a closing note for this drives lesson, remember that catalogue peak torque figures assume defined thermal and DC-bus conditions. Always cross-check continuous RMS torque for your motion profile, verify encoder cable shield grounding practice, and confirm that Safe Torque Off cuts the modulation enable path rather than merely commanding zero current in software. Those three checks prevent the majority of “mysterious” servo faults seen during first power-up of integrated BLDC systems.
