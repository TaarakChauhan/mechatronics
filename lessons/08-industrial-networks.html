Module 8 — What changed: modern systems

Industrial networks

Wiring discrete 24 V signals scaled until networks became the nervous system of machines. Understanding cycle time and jitter is now as important as understanding torque curves.

From fieldbus to Industrial Ethernet

Classic fieldbuses (PROFIBUS, DeviceNet, etc.) gave way to Ethernet-based systems: EtherCAT, PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, and others. They differ in how they schedule traffic, synchronise clocks, and map device data — but all aim for predictable I/O.

Figure: Conceptual EtherCAT line — master and daisy-chained slaves.

OPC UA, TSN, 5G/URLLC

OPC UA provides information modelling and secure sessions from machine to cloud. TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) adds bounded latency features to standard Ethernet. Cellular URLLC targets ultra-reliable low-latency links for mobile assets — still carefully engineered, not magic.

Contrast: RS-232/485 and classic CAN

RS-232 is point-to-point, short, legacy diagnostics. RS-485 enables multi-drop differential links (Modbus RTU worlds). CAN provides robust multi-master arbitration for vehicles and machines; CAN FD increases payload. They remain relevant — Ethernet did not delete every cable.

Worked example — jitter budget

A 1 kHz position loop wants sample jitter ≪ 100 µs. If network delivery varies by 400 µs, the discrete controller sees a moving plant — margins shrink. Soft real-time IT switches without TSN/industrial profiles often fail this budget.

Key idea Cycle time is the average heartbeat; jitter is the heartbeat irregularity that control loops feel as noise and delay.

Key terms

EtherCAT
Industrial Ethernet with on-the-fly processing and distributed clocks.
OPC UA
Platform-independent industrial information/exchange framework.
TSN
Ethernet features for time-sensitive traffic.

Design checklist

For a new cell: pick the motion bus for axes (often EtherCAT), a cell network for PLC/HMI, and OPC UA for upward connectivity. Document cycle times, sync method, and maximum nodes. Measure jitter on the real topology with the final switches — not on a lab hub.

Wireless URLLC may free AGVs from rails, but coexistence testing with Wi-Fi and metal structures is mandatory. Always keep a wired safety backbone.

Practical topology rules

Separate motion traffic from office IT physically or with enforced TSN/QoS. Never “temporary” share unmanaged switches.

Document every device MAC/IP/station alias. Undocumented nodes become unmaintainable nodes.

Test cable pull and EMI near welders; protocols that work on a desk fail beside arc noise without proper shielding.

Plan spare bandwidth; utilisation near 90% collapses under bursts.

Extended studio notes — 08 industrial networks

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Cycle time and jitter are control parameters carried on cables. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming office Ethernet switches are “fine for a demo” becomes a production outage. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Document topologies like you document pneumatic schematics. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Keep safety I/O on certified channels even when process data rides Ethernet. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. ROS 2 and OPC UA sit above these transports with their own QoS stories. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

1 kHz cyclic data with ±250 µs jitter is often unacceptable for precision motion; industrial profiles and distributed clocks exist to crush that jitter. Measure with hardware timestamps, not hope.
