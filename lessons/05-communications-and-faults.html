Module 5 — Embedded brains

Communications & faults

Mechatronic devices talk — and fail. Communications architecture and fault handling separate toys from machinery.

OSI idea (lightweight)

The OSI model layers responsibilities from physical bits to application meaning. You need not memorise every PDU; you do need to know whether a bug is cable integrity, framing, addressing, or application protocol mismatch.

Serial, parallel, wireless

UART/RS-232/RS-485, SPI, I²C, CAN, Ethernet, Wi-Fi, BLE, and 5G each trade distance, determinism, and complexity. Wireless simplifies moving axes but complicates coexistence and security.

Watchdogs, parity, common faults

A watchdog timer resets a runaway CPU. Parity/CRC detect corruption. Common faults: loose shields, ground potential differences, duplicate CAN IDs, unterminated RS-485, saturated actuators interpreted as “logic bugs,” and simulation that never included the failure mode you just hit.

Try this Unplug a sensor in a simulator (or mentally). Does the system fail safe, freeze last value, or race to the stops? Explicit fault policies are design deliverables.

Worked example — parity

7 data bits + parity. Payload 0b1010011 has four 1s. Even parity bit = 0; odd parity bit = 1. Receiver recomputes; mismatch flags an error — it does not correct (unlike richer codes).

Key terms

Watchdog
Timer that resets the system if not kicked in time.
CRC
Cyclic redundancy check for error detection.
Fail-safe
Fault response that moves toward a safer state.

Diagnostics mindset

When a network “sometimes” fails, think physical layer first: terminations, shield continuity, common-mode voltage, and supply dips. Then timing: overloaded buses, duplicate IDs, and mis-set baud rates. Only then chase application logic. Packet captures and error counters are your friends.

Simulation should inject faults: disconnects, stuck actuators, delayed messages. If your only test is the happy path, your first field fault is an unpaid teacher.

Fault classification

Classify faults as transient (retry), persistent (alarm and degrade), or critical (safe stop). The same CRC error may be transient on a noisy line and critical on a safety channel.

Watchdog hierarchy: task watchdogs inside an RTOS, CPU windowed watchdogs, and external hardware cutouts for outputs. One watchdog is rarely enough for high risk.

Maintain a fault dictionary with codes, operator text, and recommended action. Random hex dumps are not diagnostics.

After repair, require a deliberate reset path so intermittent faults do not silently clear without inspection.

Extended studio notes — 05 communications and faults

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Most “protocol bugs” are still connectors, shields, and power dips. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming CRC makes data correct rather than merely detected-wrong leads to silent stalls if you ignore error handlers. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Watchdogs without defined recovery states reboot into the same fault forever. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Fault dictionaries with operator language reduce mean time to repair. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. OT security later assumes you already detect integrity failures. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Even parity on 0b1101001 (four 1s) ⇒ parity bit 0 for even scheme. A single flipped data bit changes parity and flags error; a double flip may sneak through — hence CRC for serious links.
