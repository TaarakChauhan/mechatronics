Module 4 — Actuation

Mechanical actuation

Mechanical actuation transforms motor or cylinder motion into the kinematic path the task needs — linear, rotary, intermittent, or oscillating.

Motion types and kinematic chains

A kinematic chain is an assembly of links and joints. Degrees of freedom (DOF) count independent coordinates needed to specify configuration. Cams convert rotation into prescribed follower motion; gears change speed/torque; ratchets give one-way drive; belts and chains transmit over distance with compliance (belts) or positive engagement (chains/gears).

Figure: Links and joints from base to end-effector (EE).

Bearings and linear electromechanical actuators

Bearings manage loads and friction; selection affects stiffness and life. Electromechanical linear actuators package motor + screw/belt + feedback into a rod-style unit — a clean alternative to pneumatics when position control and energy use matter.

Worked example — gear ratio

Motor 3000 rpm, desired lead-screw 300 rpm → ratio 10:1. If screw lead is 5 mm/rev, linear speed = 300 rev/min × 5 mm = 1500 mm/min = 25 mm/s. Torque at screw rises ≈×10 (efficiency losses apply).

Key idea Mechanism design sets the plant the controller must face: backlash, compliance, and reflected inertia dominate achievable bandwidth.

Key terms

DOF
Degrees of freedom of a mechanism.
Cam
Profiled element producing programmed follower motion.
Reflected inertia
Load inertia as seen through gearing at the motor.

Compliance, friction, and bandwidth

Every belt, shaft, and coupling is a spring. Soft springs lower mechanical resonance and limit achievable control bandwidth. Designers sometimes stiffen structure; sometimes they notch-filter resonances; sometimes they accept lower gains. Ignoring resonance and raising gain is how machines sing.

Friction — Coulomb and viscous — creates tracking errors and limit cycles with integral control. Feedforward friction compensation and dither are classical tricks; modern drives estimate disturbance observers online.

Transmission design for control

Reflected inertia J_ref ≈ J_load / n² for ideal ratio n. High ratios make motor inertia dominate, easing control but slowing output. Low ratios make load inertia dominate and expose compliance.

Preload bearings and nuts to reduce backlash at the cost of friction and wear. Decide from positioning tolerance and reverse-motion duty.

Cam design encodes a motion law (dwell, rise, return). Polynomial and cycloidal laws trade jerk and peak acceleration. Electronics can mimic cams with electronic line shafting — shifting the problem into software and servo bandwidth.

Document lubrication and sealing; many “control problems” are dry bearings.

Extended studio notes — 04 mechanical actuation

In professional mechatronics work, the ideas in this lesson show up less as isolated definitions and more as constraints that fight each other. Mechanisms set plant resonances that no software fully erases. When you write requirements, force numbers onto those constraints: sample rates, newton-metres, degrees Celsius, milliseconds of latency, millimetres of error. Vague adjectives hide trade-offs until tooling steel is already ordered.

A useful studio habit is to keep a living “assumption list” beside your block diagram. Typical assumptions include linearity, constant temperature, perfect sampling clocks, infinite actuator authority, and noise-free sensors. Assuming zero compliance in belts invites low-frequency hunting. Crossing out an assumption should trigger either a model upgrade, a hardware change, or an explicit residual risk acceptance signed by someone accountable.

Commissioning is where theory meets cable trays. Expect ground loops, mis-labelled I/O, incorrect engineering-unit scales, and mechanical binding that looks like a “software bug.” Backlash plus integral control yields limit cycles; fix mechanics or rethink the law. Bring an oscilloscope or drive trace tool early; bring a torque wrench and alignment dial indicator just as early. The mechatronic engineer who only debugs in an IDE will misclassify half of field faults.

Documentation is part of the control system. Record firmware hashes, gain sets, mechanical revisions, and network topologies together. Future failure analysis needs that bundle. Lubrication schedules belong beside gain sets in maintenance manuals. If your organisation treats documents as after-the-fact paperwork, expect to rediscover tuning constants from scratch after every staff rotation.

Finally, connect this lesson forward and backward in the course. Foundations without modern networks leave you unable to ship connected machines; modern buzzwords without foundations leave you unable to stabilise a simple loop. Cobot compliance later is sometimes intentional mechanism design, sometimes software. Use the quizzes not as memorisation theatre but as prompts to redraw the diagrams from memory on paper — that retrieval practice builds the intuition you will need on a noisy factory floor.

Additional worked scenario

Motor inertia 2e-4 kg·m², load 0.05 kg·m², ratio 10:1 ⇒ reflected load 5e-4. Total at motor ≈7e-4. If the drive prefers load/motor inertia ratio near 1–3, you are in a reasonable zone; at ratio 1:1 absolute (n=1) the drive would see 0.05/2e-4=250 — usually painful without retuning or gearing.
